📋 Editorial Standards

Who writes this site, how the numbers are checked, and what to do when we get one wrong. | Updated 2026-11-28

Why This Site Is Signed by a Team, Not a Person

Most pet sites put a name and a headshot at the top of every article. Sometimes that person has real credentials — a DVM, a board certification, a decade in a clinic. Sometimes the name is there because a name converts better than no name.

We don't have a single named expert who can put their professional reputation behind every page here. Rather than invent one, we sign the work as an editorial team and publish the standards we actually work to. A made-up researcher with a convincing backstory is worse than no byline at all: it's a claim a reader cannot check.

So this page is the byline. Everything below is a rule we can be held to, and every one of them is something you can test by opening any guide on this site.

Three Kinds of Statements, Always Kept Apart

Every factual statement on MeltPet falls into one of three buckets, and we try never to blur them:

Measured. A number that comes from a published source, a manufacturer's own documentation, or a survey with a stated sample. It gets a citation, and the citation goes to the primary source rather than to somebody else's summary of it.

Estimated. A range we built by combining public prices, weight tables, or feeding guidelines. These are always presented as ranges, never as single numbers, and the page tells you what was combined to produce them.

Opinion. A judgement — which food format is easier to manage, whether a breed suits a first-time owner. It's written as judgement, not as fact, and it's kept out of the tables.

The distinction matters most in our calculators. A calculator that invents a midpoint to look precise is lying with a decimal point. Ours do arithmetic on numbers that appear in the article text, so you can trace any output back to a stated input.

Where the Numbers Come From

Cost figures are built from public retail listings sampled at the time the page was last updated, and the update date is printed on the page. They are retail ranges for a typical household, not quotes.

Health and nutrition claims are checked against primary veterinary and regulatory sources — the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center for toxicity, the Merck Veterinary Manual for clinical summaries, AAFCO nutrient profiles for feeding standards, and peer-reviewed literature indexed in PubMed Central where a specific claim needs a specific study.

Breed lifespans and weight ranges are drawn from breed-club and veterinary-university sources, and where those disagree we publish the disagreement instead of averaging it into a cleaner-looking number.

What We Don't Do

We don't diagnose. No page here tells you what is wrong with your animal. Guides describe what a symptom can mean and when it needs a vet today rather than next week. That's triage information, not a diagnosis.

We don't sell placements. Product links are Amazon affiliate links, disclosed at the top of every page that carries them. No brand has paid for a mention, a ranking, or a rewrite. If that ever changes, the commercial page will be labelled as commercial and kept separate from the editorial guides.

We don't publish a number we can't source. When the evidence isn't there, the page says so. A blank space is more useful than a confident guess.

Corrections

If a figure on this site is wrong, out of date, or unsourced, tell us which page and which number. Email support@meltpet.com with the URL and the claim. Corrections are made in the page itself, with the update date changed, rather than in a comment nobody will read.

We would rather be corrected than be consistent.

For corrections, source requests, or questions about a specific figure: reach out through the About page or email support@meltpet.com.
